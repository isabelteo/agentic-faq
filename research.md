
# Problem Statement

Issues are increasingly complex and inter-related, requiring multiple agencies to work together. Agency silos have been difficult to mitigate with different processes and systems implemented across the public service. For citizens, navigating the labyrinth of public services can feel like knocking on countless wrong doors, with each visit ending in polite redirection and growing frustration.

With policies of 'no wrong door' and a 'first responder agency', agencies carry the load of requests raised wrongly by citizens who were unclear who to find. Frontline officers scramble in the background to identify the most relevant agency with domain expertise, rather than focusing on critical requests that only their agency can handle.

To reduce the heavy inter-agency coordination work done by frontline officers, the government has introduced Whole-of-Government service delivery models and digital initiatives that seek to address this issue.

## Creating single touchpoints for queries and requests

Rather than having citizens decide the touchpoint agency and having each agency figure out the routing process, citizens are directed to a central touchpoint that will handle the inter-agency coordination.

This has been implemented in several ways:
- A human coordinator: Instead of families navigating multiple agencies separately, ComLink+ assigns a dedicated Family Coach as a single point of contact who helps families navigate all available social support services.
- A coordinating office: The Municipal Services Office by MND handles feedback about issues like malfunctioning corridor lights, leaky covered walkways, and littered common areas that can span multiple agencies. ServiceSG was created as well to create more one-stop physical and digital shops for citizens.
- One-stop digital shops for self-service created by coordinating offices:
   OneService by MSO LifeSG by ServiceSG handled about 38% of complaints pushed to the government in 2023 out of 1.7 million. It now has a Telegram and Chatbot integration that is able to route users to the correct agency with 85% accuracy. 
- LifeSG integrated 18 systems and 40 APIs across multiple public and private organisations to empower citizens to manage their 'Moments Of Life'. On LifeSG, new parents can perform birth registration, set up the child’s bank account, and apply for associated government benefits in just 15 minutes on their own, compared to 60 minutes previously.
- Physical helpdesks that bridge the digital divide: A series of 9 physical centers have been established by ServiceSG. Centre officers are cross trained across different services and help citizens access digital services and guide them to handle complex, inter-agency transactions. More than 306,000 citizens (with 70 per cent of them being seniors who are 55 years old or older) were assisted with more than 450,000 transactions in 2024 alone.

A major barrier to scale is the fragmented state of AI adoption across agencies. While pockets of innovation exist, according to a whitepaper by IDC and Dell, titled “Building a Leading Sovereign AI Nation,” 47% of Singapore public agencies surveyed cited lack of strategic plans and clear business cases for Sovereign AI adoption as one of the key factors holding back Sovereign AI ambitions.

Research has shown that 61% of public-sector leaders cited inadequate data infrastructure as a barrier to innovation. This is hardly surprising. Advanced AI systems require secure, interoperable, and scalable data pipelines and cannot be layered on top of outdated technology.

GovTech Singapore’s agentic AI architecture and responsible system aim to design playbooks that point agencies in the right direction.  The government can also minimize overlapping efforts and incentivize the sharing of solutions, monitoring progress through sequenced milestones.

GovTech has invested significantly in building the high-quality, contextualised government datasets needed to train and fine-tune models for public sector use cases. This data infrastructure work happens before a model goes into production, not after.

GovTech enables agencies to experiment with AI through shared platforms, pre-trained models, and technical support, lowering the barrier to AI adoption considerably. LAUNCH!, GovTech's whole-of-government AI innovation platform, lets public officers pitch ideas, prototype solutions with technical backing, and test AI applications in a supported environment. 

Singapore's public sector balances AI innovation with citizen privacy and data protection through robust governance frameworks backed by purpose-built tools. AI Guardian ↗(opens in new tab), GovTech's platform for safety testing and governance, includes two complementary products: Litmus, which assesses safety and security risks before deployment using adversarial testing, and Sentinel, which provides real-time content moderation and output filtering once applications are live. Since its launch, about one-third of all government agencies now use Litmus and Sentinel daily to safeguard their AI applications.

LionGuard 2 adds another layer of protection. GovTech's open-source multilingual content moderation guardrail covers all four of Singapore's official languages, including Singlish and code-switching, addressing gaps that content moderation systems built for Western AI contexts were not designed to handle. LionGuard 2 is deployed on AI Guardian and available as an open-source API for any text-centric large language model system.
