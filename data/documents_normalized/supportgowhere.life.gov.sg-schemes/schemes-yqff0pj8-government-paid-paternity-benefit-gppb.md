# Government-Paid Paternity Benefit (GPPB)

## Overview
Provides up to 4 weeks of income support for eligible working fathers who do not qualify for Government-Paid Paternity Leave (GPPL) due to their employment arrangement.

## Eligibility
**For natural fathers with live births:**
- Your child's date of birth/estimated delivery date is on or after 1 Jan 2021
- Your child is a Singapore Citizen or becomes one within 12 months of birth

**For natural fathers with stillbirths:**
- The date of stillbirth is on or after 1 November 2021
- Either you or the child's mother is a Singapore citizen

**For adoptive fathers:**
- You are not the natural father of the child
- Your FIA (Formal Intent to Adopt) date is on or after 1 Jan 2021
- The child must be below 12 months old on FIA date
- If the child is not a Singapore citizen, a dependent's pass (DP) has been issued and either you or your wife are Singapore citizens

**Applicable to all:**
- Employed or self-employed for at least 90 days in the 12 months before child's birth
- Lawfully married to the child's mother between conception and birth or within 12 months of birth
- Not eligible for Government-Paid Paternity Leave (GPPL) except under specific conditions such as end of employment contract or retrenchment

## How to Apply / Next Steps
- Record employment or self-employment details for the 12 months before your child's birth or Formal Intent to Adopt (FIA) date
- Submit your claim online within 15 months of your child's birth or FIA date
- An officer will contact you following your submission

## Support / Benefits
- For children born or adopted before 1 Jan 2024: up to 2 weeks of GPPB, capped at $2,500 per week
- For children born or adopted on or after 1 Jan 2024: up to 4 weeks of GPPB, capped at $2,500 per week

## Related Issues & Concerns
- Worried about losing income while taking time off after their child is born because their job doesn't offer paternity leave
- Not sure if they qualify for paternity support since they're self-employed
- Adopted a child and want to know if they can still get paternity income support
- Experienced a stillbirth and unsure what support is available
- Missed the paternity leave window due to end of contract or retrenchment and want to know what benefit they can still claim
