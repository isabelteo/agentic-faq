# Assisted Deputyship Application Programme (ADAP)

## Overview
ADAP supports caregivers of children without mental capacity to apply to be court-appointed deputies, through the simplified deputyship process. As a deputy, the caregiver can make legal decisions for the child when he/she turns 21.

## Eligibility
- Your child is between 18 and 21 years old and in the graduating cohort in a Special Education (SPED) school; or above 18 years old and enrolled in an MSF-funded Day Activity Centre (DAC) or Sheltered Workshop (SW)
- You agree to use the standard list of powers/orders available under the Integrated Family Application Management System (iFAMS) when filing a simplified Deputyship application
- All family members and any other caregivers agree for you to make the application (i.e. it is an uncontested case)

## How to Apply / Next Steps
- Caregivers can approach the relevant staff in the SPED school, Day Activity Centre or Sheltered Workshop to indicate interest to apply for Deputyship; the school or centre will work with an MSF-appointed ADAP Agency to guide the parent/caregiver in their Deputyship application
- Caregivers whose child with disability is not enrolled in a service should contact the ADAP Agency directly via phone or email for enquiries on deputyship application

## Support / Benefits
- Programme provides guidance/facilitation for caregivers of persons with no mental capacity to manage their own matters in adulthood, to apply to Family Justice Courts to be appointed as legal deputies for their child; this is a simplified process that is also more affordable compared to the standard route
- Courts may accept mental capacity assessment reports prepared by psychologists from SPED Schools, selected DACs, SWs, or the MSF-appointed ADAP Agency in lieu of a medical report (only when the child is enrolled in a SPED school, DAC, or SW onboard the ADAP programme; otherwise the assessment must be conducted by a medical doctor or psychiatrist)
