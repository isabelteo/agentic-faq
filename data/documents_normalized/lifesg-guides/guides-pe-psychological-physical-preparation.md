# Psychological Physical Preparation

## Overview
This guide helps National Service (NS) pre-enlistees prepare mentally and physically for NS, and explains the support structures available during basic training.

## Support / Benefits
- It is normal to face adjustment challenges in the first few weeks of NS; the first 2 weeks of basic training involve a transition period staying in camp under regimental routines.
- Common experiences during this period include homesickness, feeling upset about losing freedom, financial burdens, stress from daily training, stress from communal living, and relationship issues.
- Support structures include: a buddy system for peer support, regular check-ins by commanders, training in recognising physical/psychological signs of stress, and an on-site Care Hub staffed by Orientation Officers, counsellors, psychologists, and Medical Officers.
- SAF, SCDF, and SPF have zero tolerance for illegal acts such as drug abuse, vaping, and drink-driving.
- Recommended sports to prepare physically: swimming (required in basic training), cardiovascular endurance sports (jogging, cycling), rock climbing/archery/air rifle (mental strength), team sports (basketball, soccer), and martial arts (wushu, taekwondo, judo).
- Counselling helplines: SAF — 1800 278 0022 (24 hours); SCDF — 1800 286 6666 (24 hours); SPF — 1800 255 1151 (24 hours); Singapore Anti-Narcotics Association (SANA) — +65 6732 1122 / Sana@sana.org.sg (Mon–Fri, 6:00pm–9:30pm).
- Financial assistance for families facing financial obstacles is available: SAF — speak to your unit manpower officer (S1), speak to your officer during interview, or contact MINDEF Shared Services – Personnel Services Centre at 6373 1140 or 6373 1155; SCDF — submit application with supporting documents through your Platoon Commander to the Human Resource Department; SPF — apply through your course manager (Home Team Academy trainees) or Divisional Manpower Officer (Police full-time NSF in a Land Division or Staff Department).

## Related Issues & Concerns
- feeling anxious or homesick before/during the first weeks of NS
- worried about coping with regimental life and losing personal freedom
- looking for confidential counselling support during NS
- family is facing financial difficulty while I serve NS
- want to know how to physically prepare before enlistment
- not sure who to talk to if I'm struggling mentally in camp
