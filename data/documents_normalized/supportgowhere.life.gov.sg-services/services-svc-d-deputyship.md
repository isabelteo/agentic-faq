# Deputyship

## Overview
Deputyship is a legal arrangement for persons who have lost mental capacity and did not appoint a Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA), allowing a court-appointed deputy (often a family member or friend) to make certain decisions on their behalf. Fees are payable and no referral is required.

## Eligibility
- Persons who have been certified to have no mental capacity
- Anyone above 21 years of age can apply to be a Deputy, usually a family member or close friend acting in the best interest of the person who lacks capacity
- A doctor must certify that the patient lacks mental capacity to make decisions due to an impairment or disturbance in the functioning of the mind or brain
- Applicants may need to fulfil the services' in-take criteria and providers' service boundaries, if any

## How to Apply / Next Steps
- Apply to the court to seek a court order to be appointed as a Deputy
- The Family Courts hear all deputyship applications under the Mental Capacity Act
- Find out more on how to apply: https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/family/deputyship

## Related Issues & Concerns
- family member lost mental capacity and never set up a Lasting Power of Attorney
- need to make financial or medical decisions for a parent who can no longer decide for themselves
- unsure how to apply to be appointed as a deputy in court
- want to know who can act as a deputy for a person with no mental capacity
- confused about the difference between deputyship and LPA
