# Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA)

## Overview
An LPA is a legal document that allows a Donor to appoint one or more trusted persons (Donees) to make decisions on personal welfare and/or property and affairs on their behalf if they lose mental capacity.

## Eligibility
- Donors must: be 21 years old and above; have the mental capacity to make their own decisions; not be an undischarged bankrupt if making an LPA for property and affairs matters
- Donees must: be 21 years old and above; be the donor's spouse or partner, family member or relative, friend, or a licensed trust company (for property and affairs matters only); have the mental capacity to make their own decisions

## How to Apply / Next Steps
- Step 1: Decide on the type of LPA form - LPA Form 1 grants general powers to Donee(s); LPA Form 2 is for applicants who want to appoint more than 2 Donees and/or more than 1 replacement Donee, and grant specific/customised powers not in the scope of Form 1 (requires engaging a lawyer)
- Step 2: Find an LPA certificate issuer who is a medical professional accredited by the OPG, a registered psychiatrist, or a practising lawyer (excluding lawyers registered under section 36E of the Legal Profession Act 1966)
- Donor and the LPA certificate issuer must digitally sign the LPA using the Singpass app; Donees can sign electronically by accepting their appointment
- The certificate issuer must submit the LPA application to the Office of the Public Guardian (OPG) via OPG Online (OPGO), as authorised by the donor, for the LPA to be legally valid
- Apply online, or book an appointment/visit a ServiceSG Centre for in-person guidance and assistance
- An LPA certificate issuer is required to confirm the donor has the mental capacity to make an LPA

## Related Issues & Concerns
- worried about who will make decisions for me if I lose mental capacity
- want to appoint a trusted family member to manage my affairs in advance
- looking to set up legal decision-making arrangements before a medical crisis
- need to know the difference between LPA Form 1 and Form 2
- caring for an ageing parent and want to plan ahead for their mental capacity decline
